From doing to leading

Distilled from thousands of hours spent working alongside partners — and the leaders their firms asked them to become.

You have been asked — or soon will be — to lead: a sector, an office, perhaps the firm itself. It is the biggest shift of a professional career, because the skills that made you an outstanding partner — the client work, the technical mastery, the personal book — are not the skills the next role demands. Among professionals, leadership is not appointed so much as granted — and continually re-earned — by the people you lead. Leading a firm is a different job, not a bigger version of the old one.

This diagnostic assesses you against the eight capability areas that define the shift from doing to leading. It takes about 20–25 minutes. Answer as you are today, not as you intend to be — the gaps are the point. Your results identify where the transition needs your attention first. Nothing is stored as you go; when you finish, you will be invited to send your results to Emperico and can print a copy for yourself.

Be honest rather than kind to yourself. The step into leadership is the most examined move of a career — your partner group will see the gaps anyway. Better that you see them first.
